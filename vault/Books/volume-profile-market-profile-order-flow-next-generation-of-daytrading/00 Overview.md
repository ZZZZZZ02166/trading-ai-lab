# Volume Profile, Market Profile, Order Flow: Next Generation of Daytrading

*Johannes Forthmann*

[PDF pp. 1–208](../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=1)

Johannes Forthmann combines market profile, volume profile, and order flow into an intraday process. Start from the recent auction and opening conditions, map useful levels from time and executed-volume distributions, then inspect the response at those levels with footprints and delta. The examples move between futures with different liquidity, pace, and session structure.

## Core takeaways

- Market profile shows where time brackets visited a price. Volume profile shows how much executed there. Their points of control can differ.
- Prior value, an initial balance, range extension, thin zones, and tails help describe where acceptance may change.
- Fixed profiles give session anchors. Flexible profiles isolate a range, trend, or rotation and reveal when activity migrates.
- Depth, large trades, delta, and imbalances matter in sequence. Where aggressive activity stalls and when price starts making progress are separate observations.
- Failed breaks and squeezes can be studied as timed moves through a reference followed by reentry and a response. The chart figures are selected historical illustrations.

Forthmann provides the auction context for much of the footprint screen in Trader Dale's book. His TPO tails and weak highs or lows measure something different from Dale's use of unfinished business at a single footprint extreme.
