# Market context

- **Use Profiles for Location and Flow for Timing**. Forthmann starts with recent auction context, uses profiles to narrow the relevant price area, then inspects executions for timing. Market profile and volume profile address time and volume acceptance respectively. The combined method asks what happens as the reference is reached. [PDF pp. 9–13](../../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=9)
- **Main-Session Activity Shapes the Intraday Context**. The book distinguishes participants by horizon and purpose, including hedgers, longer-horizon traders, and day traders. Their activity varies by instrument, release schedule, and primary session. Forthmann treats a single participant label as an interpretation of observed behavior. [PDF pp. 14–18](../../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=14)
- **P and b Shapes Need Market Context**. A P profile can show higher acceptance after a rally or a short-covering pause in a broader decline. A b shape can likewise appear in a down move or near a developing base. Forthmann looks at what came before and how the next auction behaves. [PDF pp. 31–33](../../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=31)
- **Anchor a Flexible Profile to a Defined Auction**. Flexible profiles can cover a specific trend, rotation, or multi-day range. The start and end must correspond to a recognizable event or trade window. Using the same range for TPO and volume makes differences in acceptance easier to interpret. [PDF pp. 38–44](../../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=38)
- **Separate Accumulation From Short Follow-Up Pauses**. Forthmann draws a flexible profile over an established rotation and watches order flow if a planned reference is nearby. He distinguishes this longer formation from a shorter pause after directional initiative has already appeared. The two pictures imply different stages of an auction. [PDF pp. 55–57](../../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=55)
- **Prepare Active-Session References Before the Opening**. Forthmann reviews the prior main-session high and low, overnight or early-session extrema, recent value and POCs, and the scheduled release calendar. Preparation creates scenarios for the open. The source's timing examples should be interpreted for their specific instrument and session. [PDF pp. 95–100](../../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=95)
- **Volatility Needs Adequate Liquidity to Be Useful**. The book separates tradable expansion from volatile action with thin liquidity and poor follow-through. Its examples show long wicks, overlapping bars, and erratic swings in the latter case. Instrument and session selection matter alongside measured range. [PDF pp. 101–104](../../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=101)
- **Broadening Swings Need a Confirmed Failure**. The book's broadening-top example marks expanding highs and lows and waits for a completed structural failure. The larger shape serves as warning context, with the subsequent failed move providing more concrete evidence. Flexible profiles map reference areas around it. [PDF pp. 123–127](../../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=123)
- **Work From Higher-Timeframe Location to Footprint**. The yen and E-mini studies first locate price around an established profile or recent extreme. A smaller price/delta view then shows whether aggression gains progress, and the footprint checks for a later initiative print or imbalance. The final entry assessment follows that order. [PDF pp. 128–136](../../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=128)
- **Evaluate a False Break by Its Return**. Forthmann describes popular breakouts and reversal formations that fail, leaving traders positioned in the wrong direction. Confirmation comes from rejection, reentry, and a subsequent attempt in the opposite direction. The recognizable formation helps define where a test occurred. [PDF pp. 166–177](../../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=166)
- **Treat a Trend as Swing Progression**. Forthmann discusses preceding swing highs and lows, closes beyond them, and follow-up behavior to read a trend. A local visual pattern can look different across chart intervals. Sustained progress and a defended retest give the structure more context. [PDF pp. 195–198](../../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=195)
