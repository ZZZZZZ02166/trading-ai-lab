# Detailed Summary

*Volume Profile, Market Profile, Order Flow: Next Generation of Daytrading by Johannes Forthmann*

## Introduction and method

Forthmann places profiles and executed order flow in one workflow. A price chart supplies a broad picture, market profile shows repeated time at different prices, and volume profile shows where executed size concentrated. A footprint or delta chart helps judge the immediate encounter with a level. The opening critique of chart patterns explains why the author puts more weight on observable activity and location, though his attributions about which participants acted still need to be treated as interpretations.

[PDF pp. 1–13](../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=1)

- **Use Profiles for Location and Flow for Timing**. Forthmann starts with recent auction context, uses profiles to narrow the relevant price area, then inspects executions for timing. Market profile and volume profile address time and volume acceptance respectively. The combined method asks what happens as the reference is reached. (pp. 9–13)

## Futures and market participants

The book situates the analysis in futures, where participation, liquidity and news timing differ by market. It distinguishes hedgers and longer-horizon participants from short-term traders. Forthmann uses other-time-frame participants as a way to interpret sustained initiative or defensive activity. An actual identity, net position or motive cannot be assigned from a footprint alone. Timing and visible capacity differ across the main and out-of-hours sessions.

[PDF pp. 14–18](../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=14)

- **Main-Session Activity Shapes the Intraday Context**. The book distinguishes participants by horizon and purpose, including hedgers, longer-horizon traders, and day traders. Their activity varies by instrument, release schedule, and primary session. Forthmann treats a single participant label as an interpretation of observed behavior. (pp. 14–18)

## Market profile and initial balance

Time price opportunity counts the brackets in which a price is visited. The illustrated half-hour letters build a distribution whose widest row is a time-based point of control. The first two brackets depict the initial balance. Range extension goes beyond it, while a one-bracket row can form part of a tail. The size and direction of any extension can be assessed relative to earlier opening balances and the prevailing auction.

[PDF pp. 19–21](../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=19)

- **TPO Measures Time at Price**. A market profile marks prices visited in successive time brackets, illustrated with half-hour letters. Wider rows show repeated visits across brackets. Its control price reflects the most TPO activity, which can differ from the price with the most executed volume. (pp. 19–21)
- **Initial Balance Anchors Opening Range Extension**. The first opening brackets define an initial balance, often the first two half-hour brackets in the illustration. Trade beyond that range is an extension. Forthmann treats a relatively narrow balance as a reason to watch for expansion, and the active instrument and session matter. (pp. 20–21)
- **Single Prints Record a Brief Visit**. A single-print price row belongs to just one TPO bracket. A single-column extension at a profile extreme is a buying or selling tail in the book's terminology. This locates brief acceptance or rejection to investigate on a revisit. (pp. 20–21)

## Volume profile and control prices

Volume profile bins executed quantity by price, while normal volume bars aggregate by time. The modal price is the VPOC. A prior VPOC that remains untested is a naked POC in this book. Thin areas describe sparse business and can give a transition or boundary around a richer auction. The EUR/USD and FDAX illustrations show prior reference levels carried forward, then price behavior observed at the new encounter.

[PDF pp. 22–27](../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=22)

- **Volume Point of Control Is a Price Mode**. The VPOC is the price with the highest executed volume in the selected profile. Ordinary time-based volume shows how much traded in each time bar. A VPOC and time-based POC can therefore represent different prices in the same auction. (p. 22)
- **A Naked POC Is Still Untested**. Forthmann calls a prior control price that has not yet been revisited a naked POC. Price can later return to the level during a different auction. The actual reaction can be assessed with current profile and footprint context. (pp. 23–26)
- **Low-Volume Nodes Mark Thin Price Regions**. Low-volume nodes are local indentations in a volume profile. They can appear between more accepted areas or along a fast directional move. Forthmann watches for either a reaction at a boundary or rapid passage through a thin region. (p. 27)

## Prior value and opening acceptance

Value area contains about seventy percent of the selected activity. Its high and low connect the previous auction to an open inside, above, or below it. A short excursion outside value has a different implication from sustained trading there. Forthmann considers the prior POC a nearer reference after a return to balance. The old full-value traversal statistic is presented with qualifications and needs the exact session boundaries, touch condition, and timeframe to be tested.

[PDF pp. 28–30](../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=28)

- **Value Area Contains About Seventy Percent**. Forthmann uses the approximately 70 percent of selected activity bracketed by value area high and value area low. The reference depends on the instrument, interval, and whether the distribution is volume or time based. It provides a way to compare the current open with prior acceptance. (pp. 28–30)
- **Track Acceptance Versus Reentry at Prior Value**. The location of the open is only the first observation. Forthmann distinguishes an outside open that sustains activity beyond value from one that quickly returns inside. In balance, the prior control price can be an intermediate rotation reference. (pp. 28–30)
- **Treat Value-Area Traversal Rates as Historical Claims**. The book mentions an older 80 percent rule for traversing prior value and qualifies its use in current markets. A reproducible test needs explicit definitions for the open, the entry back into value, target touch and observation horizon. A quoted historical heuristic is only a hypothesis for the repo. (p. 30)

## D P b and double distributions

A D shape emphasizes broad middle acceptance. A P or b shape puts the bulge higher or lower. Forthmann's examples show that a P can also come from short covering during a downtrend, and a b formation may be part of a developing bottom. A double distribution records a shift from one accepted region to another across a thinner transition. Reading the shape in relation to the previous move is part of the setup.

[PDF pp. 31–33](../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=31)

- **P and b Shapes Need Market Context**. A P profile can show higher acceptance after a rally or a short-covering pause in a broader decline. A b shape can likewise appear in a down move or near a developing base. Forthmann looks at what came before and how the next auction behaves. (pp. 31–33)
- **Double Distributions Have Two Acceptance Regions**. A double distribution occurs when the market spends time or trades heavily in one region, moves through a thinner transition, and builds a second concentration. It gives two potential reference zones and a transition area. The later open and subsequent activity help tell which zone matters. (pp. 32–33)

## Fixed and flexible profiles

Daily and weekly profiles provide repeatable anchors. Main-session and 24-hour windows can put their references at different levels. A flexible profile isolates a selected rotation, impulse, or multi-day range and can be shared between the time and volume views. Forthmann studies the two control prices together or follows a developing POC as volume builds at another price. The FDAX example shows a shift after a smaller auction is established. A POC move inside sideways trade has a weaker interpretation without fresh initiative.

[PDF pp. 34–51](../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=34)

- **Compare Like Sessions Before Comparing Profiles**. Fixed profiles provide consistent daily, weekly or monthly windows. Forthmann checks main-session and 24-hour profiles separately because they can place the highest-volume node at different prices. He emphasizes recent references and uses older levels selectively. (pp. 34–38)
- **Anchor a Flexible Profile to a Defined Auction**. Flexible profiles can cover a specific trend, rotation, or multi-day range. The start and end must correspond to a recognizable event or trade window. Using the same range for TPO and volume makes differences in acceptance easier to interpret. (pp. 38–44)
- **Range Auction Can Show Two Control Prices**. Forthmann profiles a defined range using both market and volume views. A TPO POC shows where time concentrated and VPOC shows where executions concentrated. Divergence identifies two reference prices to carry into the subsequent session. (pp. 40–43)
- **Developing POC Migration Tracks Moving Acceptance**. When new trades build sufficient size at another price, the developing volume POC can migrate. Forthmann reads sustained migration in the context of an existing move and fresh initiative. He gives less weight to an isolated shift inside an established range. (pp. 45–50)

## Bounce backs at reference zones

A retest can respond ahead of a prior volume band, penetrate part of it or cross it entirely. The earlier cluster or imbalance defines a zone, while the next auction supplies the evidence for whether it holds. The examples move from prior activity into a new encounter, which allows a study to record distance, penetration and follow-through separately instead of grading a reaction by exact-tick touch.

[PDF pp. 52–54](../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=52)

- **A Profile Bounce Can Start Before the Line**. Forthmann treats strong volume and thin-profile transitions as zones. A retest may turn ahead of the original activity, react inside the band, or go through it. He uses the current response to distinguish these possibilities. (pp. 52–54)

## Accumulation follow-up and weak extremes

The author distinguishes an established rotation from a shorter follow-up pause after directional price movement begins. Both can be profiled where they occur near an existing reference. Reversals and weak highs or lows add a separate question: did price decisively reject a region or leave nearby equal extremes that could be revisited? The opening examples show sequential runs through early-session and prior-day levels. The weak-extreme usage is chart-based and should not be confused with Dale's footprint-extreme print rule.

[PDF pp. 55–60](../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=55)

- **Separate Accumulation From Short Follow-Up Pauses**. Forthmann draws a flexible profile over an established rotation and watches order flow if a planned reference is nearby. He distinguishes this longer formation from a shorter pause after directional initiative has already appeared. The two pictures imply different stages of an auction. (pp. 55–57)
- **A Weak Extreme Can Advertise a Later Sweep**. A weak high or low may consist of closely repeated prices with little decisive rejection. Forthmann treats that structure as a possible target for a later sweep. This chart-based usage of unfinished auction is distinct from a zero-quantity test inside a single footprint. (pp. 58–60)

## Hardware software and feeds

Forthmann explains the charting and data setup needed to compare price, depth, and executed order flow. The book's platform and vendor descriptions reflect its publication period. The durable requirements are sufficient intraday granularity, a clear mapping between feed and instrument, and consistent session definitions. Charting complexity is useful only to the extent that it supports the decision sequence.

[PDF pp. 61–63](../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=61)

## DOM tape and footprints

The DOM is a view of orders still resting at each price, and it can change before anything trades. Tape tools flag faster execution or larger prints. A footprint preserves executions by price and side, including diagonal comparisons and clusters of same-side imbalances. The author uses imbalances as part of a sequence, sometimes with a break in the stack, and looks for actual follow-through in prices and bar control levels. A conspicuous print after a failed extreme has a different context from the same print in the middle of a range.

[PDF pp. 64–77](../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=64)

- **DOM Displays Resting Depth at Each Price**. The DOM shows current resting limit orders around the market. Aggressive trades can consume liquidity at the best bid or ask, but resting quotes can change or disappear. Forthmann uses executed flow and price response alongside displayed depth. (pp. 64–66)
- **Tape Speed Is an Alert to Read Context**. The book uses large-print and speed-of-tape displays to call attention to an acceleration near a swing, reference zone, or stop run. The display alerts the trader to inspect the next price reaction. A burst can mark exhaustion or the start of a directional move. (pp. 67–70)
- **Footprints Time a Move at a Known Level**. A footprint decomposes each bar into executed volume per price level and side. Forthmann looks for the sequence of imbalances around a mapped reference. His examples allow the chain to contain a break and use an illustrative ratio distinct from Dale's software default. (pp. 70–73)
- **Stack Settings Depend on Platform and Instrument**. The E-mini illustration compares adjacent price rows and illustrates a two-to-one labeling example. Forthmann also allows interpretation of an imperfect stack when neighboring activity supports it. A runnable test needs to declare the ratio, adjacency, minimum size, and treatment of gaps. (pp. 71–73)
- **Confirm a Footprint Impulse With Price Progress**. Forthmann checks whether price and successive local control prices advance after a first stack or large execution. If an opposing print cannot move price through an extreme, the next impulse can clarify which side is gaining initiative. A preidentified reference gives the sequence context. (pp. 74–77)

## Delta absorption and initiative

Divergence appears when price makes less progress than signed executions imply. At a high, repeated buying may meet passive sellers while price stops advancing. At a low, selling may meet replenishing buy liquidity. The Hang Seng example combines a narrow opening balance, a defended price low, falling delta, then an upward POC crossing. The book also shows price leading delta. These cases suggest retaining the chronological order of the stall, the directional response and the eventual resolution.

[PDF pp. 78–94](../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=78)

- **Absorption Appears as Execution Without Progress**. Strong buy-initiated executions can hit passive selling while price fails to establish a new high. The mirrored case has heavy selling with a defended low. Forthmann compares price and delta to infer this condition, with replenishing liquidity as one explanation. (pp. 78–81)
- **Icebergs Are an Inference From Repeated Fills**. Hidden or replenishing liquidity can produce many executions at one level without sustained movement. Displayed DOM depth alone cannot reveal total hidden interest or the owner. The useful observation is persistent execution with a disproportionate or absent price response. (pp. 80–84)
- **Wait for Narrow Opening Balance to Resolve**. In the Hang Seng example, a narrow range forms near the active open while delta keeps falling and price holds a higher low. The later upward VPOC crossing helps confirm resolution. The example separates the initial stall from the later directional evidence. (pp. 83–85)
- **Price Sometimes Moves Before Delta Catches Up**. Forthmann shows both the standard absorption divergence and another sequence in which price has already turned while delta still lags. Compare synchronized panels, observe the next footprint imbalance, and note when the disagreement resolves. The ordering matters for any replay study. (pp. 86–90)
- **Delta Has Time and Volume-at-Price Views**. The book shows signed delta by bar, CVD and delta distributed across price levels. The views can locate stretches of related aggression or a large apparent buying or selling extreme. Comparing those prints with actual price progress is part of interpreting them. (pp. 91–94)

## Preparation and liquidity conditions

The author prepares the prior main-session reference levels, value and POC, overnight or early-session extremes, and scheduled releases before evaluating the open. The liquidity/volatility material warns about erratic movement with wide wicks and thin conditions. Historical thresholds and preferred market hours are author examples. The practical distinction is between price travel that can be planned around and unstable moves with little usable follow-through.

[PDF pp. 95–107](../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=95)

- **Prepare Active-Session References Before the Opening**. Forthmann reviews the prior main-session high and low, overnight or early-session extrema, recent value and POCs, and the scheduled release calendar. Preparation creates scenarios for the open. The source's timing examples should be interpreted for their specific instrument and session. (pp. 95–100)
- **Volatility Needs Adequate Liquidity to Be Useful**. The book separates tradable expansion from volatile action with thin liquidity and poor follow-through. Its examples show long wicks, overlapping bars, and erratic swings in the latter case. Instrument and session selection matter alongside measured range. (pp. 101–104)
- **Footprint Magnitudes Depend on the Instrument**. In choosing markets, Forthmann compares pace, session timing, liquidity and usual contract sizes. A large print in a thin contract can look small in a more active contract. Use comparable session windows before interpreting relative activity. (pp. 105–107)

## Opening types and profile tails

An opening drive is evaluated against prior balance and the path leading into the session. A directional start after consolidation differs from one after a long, crowded extension. The crude and E-mini examples mark a local range, profile tails and a false break. The smaller footprint then tests whether aggressive execution supports the recovery. This is an example of the same tool set being used at more than one horizon.

[PDF pp. 108–117](../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=108)

- **An Opening Drive Depends on the Prior Auction**. Forthmann distinguishes directional opening initiative from a test or rotation. An early push following consolidation can carry a different meaning from a push after several already extended sessions. Earlier tails, value and the new auction provide reference points. (pp. 108–112)
- **Use Profile Tails to Frame False Breaks**. The crude and E-mini examples establish a short opening range, show a break that fails, then use lower-timeframe footprints to assess the response. Existing single-print tails and value structure give the probe a defined location. A subsequent stack is interpreted in the context of that sequence. (pp. 113–117)

## Filtering the ledge

A ledge is a compressed pause inside a more pronounced move. Forthmann places a flexible volume profile over the pause and uses visible shifts in control or initiative to judge continuation. His Nasdaq example shows a P shape caused by temporary covering within a downward move, followed by another downswing. A context-sensitive profile preserves that distinction better than assigning a direction to the letter.

[PDF pp. 118–122](../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=118)

- **A Hanging Ledge Can Be a Continuation Pause**. A ledge is a compact pause inside a larger move. Forthmann profiles the ledge, examines its developing control price, and uses follow-through to distinguish continuation from an attempted reversal. A P shape can form during temporary short covering in a downtrend. (pp. 118–122)

## Broadening tops

Successive wider highs and lows give the broadening shape in the book. The author treats it as a warning and pays particular attention to a later failed break of the range after profiling the ongoing auction. The study distinction is between a formation that is identifiable in real time and a completed historical drawing that already reveals its outcome.

[PDF pp. 123–127](../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=123)

- **Broadening Swings Need a Confirmed Failure**. The book's broadening-top example marks expanding highs and lows and waits for a completed structural failure. The larger shape serves as warning context, with the subsequent failed move providing more concrete evidence. Flexible profiles map reference areas around it. (pp. 123–127)

## Complete chart examples

The examples vary in instrument and speed, but the reading sequence is consistent. The yen approaches a recent high before sell-side activity is examined inside a rise. The E-mini moves from a profile reference to one-minute divergence and a subsequent footprint confirmation. FDAX uses a prior VPOC and downside progression, Bund uses a double distribution and a slower footprint, and crude profiles separate legs during one session. Gold, EUR/USD and the weekly crude chart illustrate the handoff between a wider reference and a localized decision. The examples describe selected sequences and provide useful replay scenarios.

[PDF pp. 128–156](../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=128)

- **Work From Higher-Timeframe Location to Footprint**. The yen and E-mini studies first locate price around an established profile or recent extreme. A smaller price/delta view then shows whether aggression gains progress, and the footprint checks for a later initiative print or imbalance. The final entry assessment follows that order. (pp. 128–136)
- **Examples Combine Distinct Pieces of Auction Evidence**. FDAX uses a previous VPOC with local delta and footprint evidence, Bund uses a double distribution and later buying sequence, and crude profiles successive intraday legs. Each case applies the same sequence to different market speeds. The examples illustrate the author's process on selected trades. (pp. 137–147)
- **Weekly Profiles Can Supply Local Reference Levels**. Forthmann projects a weekly VPOC onto a faster crude chart to observe the next interaction. The gold and EUR/USD examples similarly use earlier structure before zooming into delta and footprints. The higher-timeframe reference and lower-timeframe trigger are separate inputs. (pp. 149–156)

## Summary and practice questions

Forthmann reviews the value of learning an instrument's pace and developing a repeatable preparation process. He distinguishes theoretical chart rules from observations about where transactions and rotations took place. The bonus questions cover setup, preparation, journaling and the limits of following other traders' opinions. For study, keep the planned setup conditions separate from what later happened.

## Fake moves and liquidity sweeps

This section groups failed breakouts, head-and-shoulders failures, backfire patterns, and squeezes. Across the examples, a recognizable level attracts activity, price reaches or exceeds it, and the expected continuation fails. The author watches early-session and prior-day extremes as common references in the illustrations. Profile context, a return through the level, and a later change in aggression provide a basis for replaying the sequence without assigning intent to every move.

[PDF pp. 166–194](../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=166)

- **Evaluate a False Break by Its Return**. Forthmann describes popular breakouts and reversal formations that fail, leaving traders positioned in the wrong direction. Confirmation comes from rejection, reentry, and a subsequent attempt in the opposite direction. The recognizable formation helps define where a test occurred. (pp. 166–177)
- **Track Prior and Overnight Extremes for Sweeps**. The book shows squeezes and prior-day or non-institutional highs and lows getting taken during the main session. The useful observation is how price responds after the extreme is reached, with profile and order flow supplying context. A sweep by itself only identifies the path through the level. (pp. 178–193)

## Trend as swing progression

The discussion focuses on price closing through meaningful prior swing points and its ability to maintain progress thereafter. The EUR/USD chart shows acceleration when preceding tops are crossed. Pullbacks and follow-up signals have meaning relative to the sequence of swings. That framing is more stable across bar sizes than a shape named from one snapshot.

[PDF pp. 195–198](../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=195)

- **Treat a Trend as Swing Progression**. Forthmann discusses preceding swing highs and lows, closes beyond them, and follow-up behavior to read a trend. A local visual pattern can look different across chart intervals. Sustained progress and a defended retest give the structure more context. (pp. 195–198)

## Trade and risk management

Before entry, identify meaningful invalidation and consider the difference between a fast low-volume region and a busy area where price can pause. The author discusses how failure to build an expected new swing changes the trade premise and emphasizes limiting losses on an intraday timescale. The examples are about the relationship of structure, available liquidity, and time in the trade.

[PDF pp. 199–203](../../../books/Volume%20Profile%20%26%20Market%20Profile%20Order%20Flow%20for%20Next%20Gen%20Daytrading.pdf#page=199)

- **Tie Invalidation to Failed Price Progress**. The book uses structure and thinner profile zones in location and risk decisions. Failure to make expected swing progress can call the trade premise into question. Forthmann emphasizes timely exits and prepares the trade plan before the decision point. (pp. 199–203)
