# Concept: The Market-Making Edge
*From: Option Market Making, Allen Jan Baird*

---

## Definition

The **market-making edge** (or "the edge") is the profit a market maker earns from buying at the bid and selling at the offer. It is the bid/offer spread applied to each completed round-turn (buy and sell of the same position).

If the bid is $1.70 and the offer is $1.80, the edge per round-turn is $0.10 per option. Multiply by contract size for dollar value.

---

## What Makes Edge Sustainable

1. **Relative pricing accuracy.** The market maker must know fair value well enough to set a bid/offer that is centered on it — not so wide that no one trades, not so narrow that they lose on every trade.

2. **Volume.** Edge per trade is small — profitability requires high volume.

3. **Risk management.** Unhedged positions can wipe out many rounds of edge in a single adverse move. Every edge-earning trade must be properly hedged.

4. **Delta neutrality.** A market maker who takes directional bets is speculating — if they're wrong, the directional loss can exceed many rounds of edge income.

---

## What Erodes Edge

- **Exchange fees and overhead.** Exchange membership costs, clearing fees, and data services come directly off gross spread income.
- **Adverse selection.** Informed traders (who know something the market maker doesn't) consistently buy calls before rallies or puts before drops. Market makers lose edge on these trades.
- **Edge giveaway.** Sometimes market makers must trade at prices better than quoted to attract order flow — giving up some edge to compete.
- **Overtrading.** Trading more contracts than you can manage properly leads to hedging errors and missed adjustments that eat edge.

---

## Tracking the Edge

Use the **two-account system** (Chapter 8):
- **Daily trading P&L** = direct measure of edge captured on new trades
- **Carryover P&L** = mark-to-market change on existing positions

A genuine market maker has positive daily trading P&L consistently, regardless of carryover. If total profits come from lucky carryover moves, you are speculating, not market making.
