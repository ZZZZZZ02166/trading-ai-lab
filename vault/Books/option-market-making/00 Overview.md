## What This Book Is About

*Option Market Making* is a practical guide to the business of being a professional option market maker in financial and commodity options markets. Unlike most options books that focus on speculative trading strategies, this book adopts the perspective of the dealer — the market maker who stands ready to buy and sell options at all times, earning income from the bid/offer spread rather than from directional bets.

The book is simultaneously a reference manual on option risk and a practical guide to running a market-making operation. It is written from the direct professional experience of an active exchange floor trader.

**Who it's for:** Professional option market makers, floor traders, options dealers at banks and securities firms, and serious options traders who want to understand the mechanics of how options are priced and risk-managed by the professionals who make markets in them.

---

## Main Themes

- **Market making as a business:** The market maker's income comes from providing liquidity (capturing the bid/offer spread), not from directional speculation. All risk management decisions flow from this core principle.

- **The six Greek risks:** Every option position carries delta, gamma, theta, kappa/vega, rho, and skew risk. Each must be understood, measured, and managed independently. The book provides unusually clear explanations of each.

- **Position risk profiles:** Any complex carryover position a market maker accumulates will resemble one of 22 core single-month option position types. Understanding these archetypes lets a market maker instantly classify and manage any portfolio.

- **Volatility as the central unknown:** Of all the inputs to option pricing models, volatility is the only one that must be estimated. The book devotes significant attention to historical vs implied volatility, their divergence, and the practical consequences.

- **Common pitfalls:** The book explicitly identifies the four most common market maker mistakes (failing to hedge delta, hedging incorrectly, overtrading, poor vega management) and explains how to avoid them.

## Most Important Takeaways

1. **Pure market making is risk-neutral** — income comes from the spread, not direction. The moment you delay a delta hedge hoping for a better level, you are speculating, not market making.

2. **Option risk is multi-dimensional** — options respond to five independent variables (price, strike, time, volatility, interest rate). Managing only delta is insufficient; gamma, theta, and kappa/vega can all cause large losses independently.

3. **Fat tails are real** — empirical data on stock, bond, and commodity price changes consistently shows fatter tails and higher peaks than the log-normal distribution assumes. BSM systematically underprices tail risk. Market makers should price wings accordingly.

4. **Implied volatility ≠ future realized volatility** — IV is the market's forward estimate, not a reliable forecast. Historical vol is a backward-looking measure. Neither reliably predicts future vol. This creates both risk and opportunity.

5. **The 22 position archetypes** — every carryover position a market maker holds will behave like one of the 22 core positions (long call, short straddle, butterfly, condor, ratio spread, etc.) in terms of gamma/theta/kappa risk profile.

6. **Hedge when you should, not when it's convenient** — the cardinal rule of market making. Delaying delta hedges to "let the market come to you" is speculation. Immediate, disciplined hedging is the foundation of sustainable market-making.

## How It Relates to Other Books

This book is the most market-maker-centric options book in this vault. Where Natenberg (*Option Volatility and Pricing*) covers similar Greek risk material from a broader trading perspective, Baird's focus is specifically on the dealer function — providing immediacy, managing carryover positions, tracking P&L attribution, and avoiding the specific operational pitfalls of floor trading. It complements volatility-focused books by grounding abstract pricing theory in the practical mechanics of running an options book.
