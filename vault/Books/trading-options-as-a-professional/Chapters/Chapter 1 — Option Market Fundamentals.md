# Chapter 1 — Option Market Fundamentals
*Source: Trading Options as a Professional — Ch. 1*

---

## Summary

Chapter 1 establishes the vocabulary and mechanics that the rest of the book builds on. Unlike most introductory chapters, Bittman assumes you've traded options before — this chapter is a professional review, not a beginner's guide. The emphasis is on institutional mechanics: how markets function, what market makers do, and how margin and short stock rebate affect option pricing.

The most important practical insight is the seven-part option order vs. four-part stock order. The three extra dimensions (expiration, strike, call vs. put) create three more ways to be right or wrong. Professional traders keep all seven dimensions in mind simultaneously.

---

## Key Concepts

### Seven-Part Option Order
Stock order: Action / Quantity / Name / Price (4 parts)  
Option order: Action / Quantity / Underlying / Expiration / Strike / Type / Price (7 parts)

Each additional dimension is an additional source of P/L variation. Option traders who only think about direction are ignoring 4 of 7 inputs.

### Intrinsic Value and Time Value
- **Intrinsic value**: in-the-money amount (max of 0 vs. stock-strike gap)
- **Time value**: everything above intrinsic
- Deep ITM options trade "at parity" (zero time value) — their price equals their intrinsic value
- OTM options are 100% time value (zero intrinsic)

### Moneyness
| Call | Stock vs. Strike |
|------|-----------------|
| ITM | Stock > Strike |
| ATM | Stock ≈ Strike |
| OTM | Stock < Strike |

For puts, the signs are reversed: ITM when stock < strike.

### Public Traders vs. Market Makers
- Public traders: outside the exchange, no quoting obligation
- Market makers: must quote both bid and ask; earn the bid-ask spread for providing liquidity
- NBBO (National Best Bid and Offer): customer orders must be filled at the best available price across all exchanges

---

## Key Lessons

1. **Margin requirements** reflect assignment risk for short options. Initial margin > maintenance margin — a drop below maintenance triggers a margin call.
2. **Short stock rebate** is income professional traders receive from the interest generated by short stock proceeds (typically 80%). This income makes certain arbitrage strategies more profitable for market makers vs. retail traders — and narrows the apparent "riskless" profit margins when you price from a retail perspective.
3. Option P/L diagrams (long call, short call, long put, short put, straddle, strangle, spreads) visually encode risk/reward. The curved prior-to-expiry lines reveal theta decay; the straight expiration line shows the final payout.

---

## Warnings

⚠️ Traders who think about option trades as "just a directional bet" are ignoring expiration, strike selection, and implied volatility — three of seven dimensions. All three can override a correct directional view.

⚠️ Short stock rebate creates a systematic pricing difference between retail and professional traders on arbitrage strategies. Retail traders should not expect to replicate market maker arbitrage profitability.
