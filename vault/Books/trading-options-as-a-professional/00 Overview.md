# Trading Options as a Professional — Overview
*James B. Bittman — Quick Overview*

---

## What the Book Is About

*Trading Options as a Professional: Techniques for Market Makers and Experienced Traders* is a comprehensive, technically rigorous textbook written for two audiences: (1) market makers in training who need a complete grounding in options mechanics, and (2) experienced traders who want to raise their game to professional level. Bittman spent years at the Options Institute of the Chicago Board Options Exchange, and this book reflects that institutional background.

Unlike introductory options books that explain calls and puts, this one assumes you already know the basics. Its purpose is to teach you to think about options the way a professional does: in terms of theoretical value, Greeks, synthetic relationships, arbitrage constraints, and volatility forecasting.

---

## Main Themes

- **Greeks as the language of risk**: The five Greeks (delta, gamma, vega, theta, rho) are not just theoretical measures — they are practical tools for calculating exact dollar P/L under any scenario. Bittman teaches all five in rigorous detail, including position Greeks scaled to full portfolio size.

- **Synthetic relationships and put-call parity**: Every option position can be replicated by a combination of the other two instruments (stock, calls, puts). Understanding this unlocks arbitrage strategies and reveals when any given form of a position is cheapest.

- **Arbitrage as price enforcer**: Conversions, reverse conversions, and box spreads are the mechanisms by which market makers keep prices in line with put-call parity. Understanding these keeps a retail trader from being on the wrong side of obvious mispricings.

- **Volatility trading**: The book distinguishes clearly between historical (realized) vol, implied vol, and a trader's personal expected vol forecast. Every option trade is ultimately a bet on this relationship. Delta-neutral strategies isolate that bet.

- **Position risk management**: Managing a portfolio of options means tracking position Greeks across the entire book and setting dollar-denominated risk limits. This is the professional skill that prevents catastrophic surprises.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. Option trading requires a **three-part forecast**: direction, timing, and volatility. Getting direction right but being wrong on timing or vol still loses money.
2. The gamma-theta tradeoff is inescapable: you cannot collect theta without accepting negative gamma, and vice versa.
3. **Delta-neutral trading is not risk-free** — it replaces directional risk with volatility risk. Profits come from being right about whether realized vol exceeds implied vol.
4. Market makers earn the bid-ask spread through active delta hedging, not by predicting direction.
5. **Position Greeks change** as market conditions change — a static Greek snapshot is not enough. Traders must understand how Greeks will evolve when the stock moves.
