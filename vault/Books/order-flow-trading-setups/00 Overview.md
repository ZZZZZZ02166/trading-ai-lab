# Order Flow Trading Setups

*Trader Dale*

[PDF pp. 1–161](../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=1)

Trader Dale uses futures footprints to examine how price reacts at predefined support or resistance. He starts with the mechanics of the bid and ask, then builds two kinds of examples: places to watch, such as heavy volume clusters and repeated nodes, and behavior to look for when price arrives, such as stalled aggression or a delta flip.

## Core takeaways

- A footprint tells you where and on which side trading was initiated. Price progress helps distinguish initiative from possible passive absorption.
- A cluster or repeated node becomes a prospective reference after price has moved away. Several of the setups specifically concern the first retest.
- An imbalance is a relative, diagonal comparison. A stack is an adjacent sequence of same-side imbalances. Neither tells you on its own why the trade occurred.
- Unfinished business is a specific print condition at a footprint extreme. Treat it as a level for study when considering nearby exits or retests.
- A flexible volume profile sets the broader location, while the footprint and cumulative-delta views examine the local response.

Dale's systematic vocabulary pairs with Forthmann's emphasis on the opening auction, prior value, session selection, and follow-through. The books' displayed trade examples illustrate their methods. The repository's saved QQQ results concern a different set of inputs and outcomes.
