# Trade confirmation

- **Delta Can Diverge From Candle Direction**. An up bar with negative delta or a down bar with positive delta shows price and executed aggression are telling different stories. Dale uses this discrepancy to investigate a reaction near a planned level. The example pairs an advancing price with weaker delta at resistance. [PDF pp. 28–30](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=28)
- **Use Separate Views for Context and Confirmation**. Dale's workspace pairs slower footprints for setup location with a faster footprint and a separate cumulative-delta view. The session profile gives a broader map for the same market. Each panel answers a different question about location or timing. [PDF pp. 44–45](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=44)
- **Treat Unfinished Business as a Nearby Reference**. Dale uses an unfinished extreme in target planning, stop placement, and trade screening. Its presence can support a revisit hypothesis when price is already near it, but the market may first move far away. The reference alone gives no dependable entry timing. [PDF pp. 71–74](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=71)
- **Choose a Reference Before Reading Confirmation**. Dale first identifies support or resistance with a primary method, then looks at order flow when price arrives. The four confirmation families are large passive orders, absorption, aggressive orders with delta, and cumulative-delta divergence. His presented confirmation setups depend on the location. [PDF pp. 80–81](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=80)
- **Infer Passive Interest From a Stalled Extreme**. At resistance a large ask print can accompany aggressive buying into a passive seller while price fails to extend. At support the mirrored example shows large bid volume during a stalled sell push. Unusually large is relative to the instrument and the session. [PDF pp. 81–86](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=81)
- **Watch Heavy Two-Sided Volume Where Price Stalls**. Dale's absorption setup looks for unusually heavy traded volume on both bid and ask around preselected support or resistance, while the previous push stalls. The evidence may take several minutes to appear. It is strongest as a response to a level already identified. [PDF pp. 87–91](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=87)
- **Look for Aggression Away From the Reference**. Dale watches for dominant bid-side execution and negative delta at resistance, or dominant ask-side execution and positive delta at support. He reads the response within five-minute footprints in these examples. The proposed aggression is in the direction of the reaction away from the level. [PDF pp. 92–95](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=92)
- **Absorption and Initiative Form Different Stages**. Dale describes a sequence in which passive interest first stalls a move and later aggressive traders push price away. The second stage supplies directional follow-through beyond the first stall. The footprint examples mark both stages separately. [PDF pp. 95–98](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=95)
- **Read Cumulative-Delta Divergence Around Planned Levels**. Dale's long example compares falling price with rising cumulative delta near support, with the mirror case near resistance. The mismatch is evaluated where the primary strategy already identified a meaningful zone. The hoped-for resolution belongs to the chart example and needs its own outcome test. [PDF pp. 99–104](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=99)
- **Profile the Rotation Before a Directional Departure**. Dale profiles the pre-move range and highlights its heaviest traded band once price breaks out. A later return to that band becomes a support or resistance candidate. The book pairs the return with a separate order-flow confirmation. [PDF pp. 130–136](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=130)
