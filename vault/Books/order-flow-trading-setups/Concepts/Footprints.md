# Footprints

- **Footprints Need Venue-Level Executed Volume**. Centralized futures data lets the analyst observe executed size by price and side for that contract. Spot FX is decentralized, so a feed from one broker gives only a partial view of spot activity. Dale uses currency futures for analysis and maps the levels separately when executing in spot. [PDF pp. 17–19](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=17)
- **Aggressive and Passive Orders Share Each Print**. A market buy executes against a resting sell limit at the ask. A market sell executes against a resting buy limit at the bid. The print locates the transaction and its aggressive side, while the price response helps infer whether the passive side mattered. [PDF pp. 20–24](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=20)
- **You Cannot Identify the Trader From the Print**. A conspicuous ask-side execution could reflect buying initiative or the ability of a passive seller to absorb it. The number identifies the traded size, and the price response supplies separate information. Dale treats participant identity and intent as inferences. [PDF pp. 24–25](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=24)
- **The Footprint Shows Executions at Each Price**. A footprint keeps the candle's price range and layers executed bid and ask quantity onto each price row. This reveals where within the bar activity was concentrated. A normal candle only shows the aggregate price movement. [PDF pp. 25–26](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=25)
- **A Footprint Node Is Its Busiest Price Row**. Dale defines a bar's high-volume node by the largest combined bid and ask volume at a single price. This is a local, per-bar reference. A different time window can produce a different node. [PDF pp. 26–27](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=26)
- **Bar Delta Is Executed Ask Minus Bid**. Bar delta sums ask-side volume and subtracts bid-side volume across the footprint. A positive value indicates more buy-initiated execution, though the candle can still close lower. Rotations can make the relationship between delta and candle direction less reliable. [PDF pp. 27–30](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=27)
- **Daily Profile Adds Context to a Footprint**. Dale displays the day's volume distribution beside individual footprints to see where repeated trading took place. The daily histogram summarizes the session by price while the footprint shows how that activity was distributed within particular bars. [PDF pp. 31–32](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=31)
- **Relative Volume Defines a Cluster**. Dale's footprint shades rows based on executed size so unusually heavy areas stand out from nearby prices. A cluster is a band of relatively heavy trading. Interpretation depends on where the cluster forms and how price subsequently leaves it. [PDF pp. 34–35](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=34)
- **Multiple Nodes Share One Price Across Bars**. A double or triple node forms when the maximum-volume row repeats at the same price in successive footprints. A coincident level in separated bars is a different measurement. Dale treats this repetition as an area to watch when price returns. [PDF pp. 35–36](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=35)
- **Footprint Imbalance Uses a Diagonal Comparison**. Dale's default imbalance compares executions on one side with the opposing volume at the neighboring price and highlights ratios of 3 to 1 or higher. The diagonal convention differs from whether one side is larger within a single row. The software ratio is configurable. [PDF pp. 36–37](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=36)
- **A Stack Groups Neighboring Same-Side Imbalances**. Adjacent imbalance levels on the same side form a stack. Dale describes the usual display as three or more, while the trading instructions allow the configured trigger to vary. The stack highlights a band of unusually one-sided execution. [PDF pp. 37–38](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=37)
- **Unfinished Business Is a Footprint-Extreme Test**. Dale expects zero executed bid volume at a finished footprint high and zero executed ask volume at a finished low. He marks an extreme with prints on both sides as unfinished business. The mark is a level to track, and revisits can take time or fail to occur. [PDF pp. 38–41](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=38)
- **Use Separate Views for Context and Confirmation**. Dale's workspace pairs slower footprints for setup location with a faster footprint and a separate cumulative-delta view. The session profile gives a broader map for the same market. Each panel answers a different question about location or timing. [PDF pp. 44–45](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=44)
- **Require Separation Before a Multiple-Node Retest**. Dale looks for at least two consecutive bar nodes at the same price in a trend, pre-trend region, or strong rejection. His setup then waits for two entire footprints to form away from the node and considers its first later revisit. The approach determines the proposed side. [PDF pp. 55–59](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=55)
- **Track Retests of Filtered Large Execution Areas**. Identify a large execution with the trades filter, then wait for one or two entire footprints to build outside the marked area. Measure the first pullback to the zone after that separation. Dale uses the approach direction to distinguish support from resistance. [PDF pp. 60–64](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=60)
- **Choose a Reference Before Reading Confirmation**. Dale first identifies support or resistance with a primary method, then looks at order flow when price arrives. The four confirmation families are large passive orders, absorption, aggressive orders with delta, and cumulative-delta divergence. His presented confirmation setups depend on the location. [PDF pp. 80–81](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=80)
- **Watch Heavy Two-Sided Volume Where Price Stalls**. Dale's absorption setup looks for unusually heavy traded volume on both bid and ask around preselected support or resistance, while the previous push stalls. The evidence may take several minutes to appear. It is strongest as a response to a level already identified. [PDF pp. 87–91](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=87)
