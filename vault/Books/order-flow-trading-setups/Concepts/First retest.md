# First retest

- **Multiple Nodes Share One Price Across Bars**. A double or triple node forms when the maximum-volume row repeats at the same price in successive footprints. A coincident level in separated bars is a different measurement. Dale treats this repetition as an area to watch when price returns. [PDF pp. 35–36](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=35)
- **Retest a Footprint Cluster Inside a Trend**. Find relatively heavy trading inside a decisive directional leg, then observe whether price separates from the band. Dale waits for a pullback to its initial boundary or heavier portion. He aligns the setup with the direction of the leg. [PDF pp. 46–50](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=46)
- **Defending Inventory Can Explain a Cluster Retest**. Dale offers two explanations for a reaction at a prior cluster: earlier participants may defend their positions, and opposing traders may close as price approaches. Both are explanations for the observed reaction. The chart directly shows concentration, departure, return, and response. [PDF pp. 47–48](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=47)
- **Profile the Cluster Inside a Sharp Rejection**. Look for a fast move that reverses decisively, then locate relatively heavy trading inside the rejection. Track the band after price clearly leaves it and test the first return in the direction of rejection. The move can span more than one candle. [PDF pp. 51–54](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=51)
- **Require Separation Before a Multiple-Node Retest**. Dale looks for at least two consecutive bar nodes at the same price in a trend, pre-trend region, or strong rejection. His setup then waits for two entire footprints to form away from the node and considers its first later revisit. The approach determines the proposed side. [PDF pp. 55–59](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=55)
- **Track Retests of Filtered Large Execution Areas**. Identify a large execution with the trades filter, then wait for one or two entire footprints to build outside the marked area. Measure the first pullback to the zone after that separation. Dale uses the approach direction to distinguish support from resistance. [PDF pp. 60–64](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=60)
- **Trade Stacked Imbalance Retests With Directional Context**. A stack during a one-way move defines a candidate band. Dale waits for price to move away before it comes back and uses only the first test in this setup. A buying stack approached from above is considered possible support, and the mirrored case possible resistance. [PDF pp. 65–70](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=65)
