# Trade management

- **Treat Unfinished Business as a Nearby Reference**. Dale uses an unfinished extreme in target planning, stop placement, and trade screening. Its presence can support a revisit hypothesis when price is already near it, but the market may first move far away. The reference alone gives no dependable entry timing. [PDF pp. 71–74](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=71)
- **Check Unfinished Extremes Before Placing Stops**. Dale checks whether a nearby unfinished extreme lies just beyond a proposed protective stop. The point is to understand whether a test of that extreme would also invalidate the chosen positioning. It remains secondary to the main setup and risk distance. [PDF pp. 73–79](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=73)
- **Aim at the Near Edge of Prior Volume**. When a trade approaches a zone with heavy historical activity, Dale considers taking profit before or at its leading edge. The zone may interrupt the move. A target selected from that distribution inherits the profile's window and price bins. [PDF pp. 105–109](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=105)
- **Reassess a Trail When Order Flow Turns**. Dale trails a favorable position while aggressive execution still supports the move. Opposing imbalance, renewed absorption, or a delta reversal near a significant price can change that read. The decision is conditioned on price location and progress. [PDF pp. 110–114](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=110)
- **Place Stops Beyond the Price Structure**. The book compares a fixed stop, a stop beyond the support or resistance extreme, and a stop beyond a heavy-volume band. The structural approaches tie the stop to the trade premise. Dale checks whether the resulting distance is sensible for the instrument and market conditions. [PDF pp. 115–120](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=115)
