# Volume profile

- **Daily Profile Adds Context to a Footprint**. Dale displays the day's volume distribution beside individual footprints to see where repeated trading took place. The daily histogram summarizes the session by price while the footprint shows how that activity was distributed within particular bars. [PDF pp. 31–32](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=31)
- **Aim at the Near Edge of Prior Volume**. When a trade approaches a zone with heavy historical activity, Dale considers taking profit before or at its leading edge. The zone may interrupt the move. A target selected from that distribution inherits the profile's window and price bins. [PDF pp. 105–109](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=105)
- **Place Stops Beyond the Price Structure**. The book compares a fixed stop, a stop beyond the support or resistance extreme, and a stop beyond a heavy-volume band. The structural approaches tie the stop to the trade premise. Dale checks whether the resulting distance is sensible for the instrument and market conditions. [PDF pp. 115–120](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=115)
- **Volume Profile Plots Executed Size by Price**. A horizontal histogram bins executions by price across a selected interval. The thick portions show where more traded, whereas ordinary volume bars total activity by time interval. The profile identifies candidate price zones for further assessment. [PDF pp. 121–124](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=121)
- **Profile Shape Describes Where Business Concentrated**. Dale associates D with a broad middle distribution, P with a higher bulge, lowercase b with a lower bulge, and thin profiles with directional travel. Each shape summarizes where volume accumulated. The time window affects which shape is visible. [PDF pp. 125–128](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=125)
- **Profile the Rotation Before a Directional Departure**. Dale profiles the pre-move range and highlights its heaviest traded band once price breaks out. A later return to that band becomes a support or resistance candidate. The book pairs the return with a separate order-flow confirmation. [PDF pp. 130–136](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=130)
- **Profile a Pause Within an Established Trend**. A strong directional move can leave a thin distribution with a relatively large bump where it paused. Dale focuses on that local concentration for a potential continuation retest. The underlying trend and the actual response at the band are both part of his setup. [PDF pp. 137–140](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=137)
- **Profile the Whole Rejection to Find the Fight**. Apply a flexible profile over a strong rejected probe and its reversal to find the heaviest traded band inside the event. This can expose levels hidden by different candle time frames. Dale describes the method as harder to apply when several concentrations compete. [PDF pp. 141–145](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=141)
