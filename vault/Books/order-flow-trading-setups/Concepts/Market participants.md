# Market participants

- **Footprints Need Venue-Level Executed Volume**. Centralized futures data lets the analyst observe executed size by price and side for that contract. Spot FX is decentralized, so a feed from one broker gives only a partial view of spot activity. Dale uses currency futures for analysis and maps the levels separately when executing in spot. [PDF pp. 17–19](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=17)
- **Aggressive and Passive Orders Share Each Print**. A market buy executes against a resting sell limit at the ask. A market sell executes against a resting buy limit at the bid. The print locates the transaction and its aggressive side, while the price response helps infer whether the passive side mattered. [PDF pp. 20–24](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=20)
- **You Cannot Identify the Trader From the Print**. A conspicuous ask-side execution could reflect buying initiative or the ability of a passive seller to absorb it. The number identifies the traded size, and the price response supplies separate information. Dale treats participant identity and intent as inferences. [PDF pp. 24–25](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=24)
- **Defending Inventory Can Explain a Cluster Retest**. Dale offers two explanations for a reaction at a prior cluster: earlier participants may defend their positions, and opposing traders may close as price approaches. Both are explanations for the observed reaction. The chart directly shows concentration, departure, return, and response. [PDF pp. 47–48](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=47)
- **A Large-Trade Filter Misses Split Execution**. The filter highlights single executions above a configurable size. Dale's 25-lot illustration is calibrated to his currency-futures example, and activity varies by instrument and session. Small clips and iceberg replenishment can be missed by a large-print cutoff. [PDF pp. 60–61](../../../../books/Order-Flow-Trading-Setups-1.pdf#page=60)
