# Trading Option Greeks 2e — Overview
*Dan Passarelli, 2012 (Second Edition)*

---

## What the Book Is About

*Trading Option Greeks* is a practitioner's guide to understanding how the five greek risk measures—delta, gamma, theta, vega, and rho—drive option prices and shape trading outcomes. Rather than a "how-to" of which trades to put on, Passarelli frames it as a "how-come" tutorial: if you understand *why* options behave the way they do, better trading decisions follow naturally.

The book is structured in three parts:
1. **The Basics** (Ch. 1–8): Options fundamentals, each greek explained in depth, put-call parity, synthetics, and the roles of interest rates and dividends.
2. **Spreads** (Ch. 9–11): Vertical spreads, wing spreads (condors/butterflies), and calendar/diagonal spreads—all analysed through a greek lens.
3. **Volatility** (Ch. 12–17): Delta-neutral trading, gamma scalping, volatility chart patterns, straddles/strangles, ratio spreads, and a final synthesis chapter.

**Who it is for**: Option traders at the intermediate level who already know what calls and puts are but have not yet developed fluency in the greeks. It is also a strong refresher for more experienced traders who want a structured, example-driven framework.

## Main Themes

- **Greeks are a risk visibility tool, not a trading system.** Delta, gamma, theta, vega, and rho translate abstract option positions into concrete, manageable numbers. Without greek literacy, option trading is guesswork.

- **The gamma-theta tradeoff is the core tension.** Every option position sits somewhere on a spectrum from long-gamma/negative-theta (pay daily for the right to profit from big moves) to short-gamma/positive-theta (collect daily income, accept the risk of large moves). Every strategy discussed in the book is a variation on how to navigate this tradeoff.

- **Implied volatility is the central variable.** IV is what the market is actually pricing—not direction. Delta-neutral strategies isolate it. Vol charts reveal when it is cheap or expensive. Straddles and strangles express pure vol views. Spreads reduce it.

- **Put-call parity connects everything.** The mathematical relationship between puts, calls, and the underlying underlies synthetics, conversions, box spreads, and every arbitrage discussed in the book. Passarelli's mantra: "A put is a call; a call is a put."

- **Every trade is a risk management decision.** Would I Do It Now? Rule. Defining max loss before entry. Understanding what the greeks look like across all possible underlying prices before putting on a position.

---

## Most Important Takeaways

1. **Delta is not just directionality.** It is also equivalent-shares exposure, probability of expiry ITM, and the slope of the option value curve. Thinking in these four simultaneous definitions is the beginning of greek fluency.

2. **Gamma and theta are always paired.** You cannot increase your theta income without simultaneously increasing your gamma risk. There is no such thing as a "free" option position that collects decay without accepting movement risk.

3. **Volatility charts are the primary analytical tool.** Nine patterns of IV vs. realized vol combinations tell you exactly what kind of vol trade (if any) to put on. Reading these charts is Passarelli's answer to "how do I find trades?"

4. **Spreads don't just reduce risk—they reshape it.** A vertical spread versus an outright option isn't just cheaper; it has 95%+ less vega and gamma. You are paying for a fundamentally different risk profile, not just a cheaper version of the same bet.

5. **Stops don't work as expected for mean-reverting strategies.** Understanding gamma makes clear that stops for short-vol positions often eliminate trades that would have recovered—they create bimodal outcome distributions, not clean truncation.

## How It Relates to Other Books in the Vault

- **vs. Positional Option Trading (Sinclair)**: Passarelli focuses on mechanics and execution; Sinclair focuses on edge, backtesting, and quantitative rigor. Both emphasise the variance premium but from very different angles—Passarelli from a practitioner narrative perspective, Sinclair from a statistical edge framework.
- **vs. Option Volatility and Pricing (Natenberg)**: Natenberg covers similar ground but with more mathematical depth. Passarelli is more narrative and example-driven; Natenberg is more reference-style. Both are foundational; Passarelli is the better first read.
- **vs. Volatility Trading (Sinclair, 1st ed.)**: Sinclair's earlier work is more model-driven. Passarelli is more accessible and trade-execution oriented.
