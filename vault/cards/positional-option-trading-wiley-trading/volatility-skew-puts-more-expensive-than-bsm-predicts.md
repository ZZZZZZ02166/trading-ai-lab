# Volatility Skew: Puts More Expensive Than BSM Predicts

**Type:** volatility_insight
**Source:** Positional Option Trading (Wiley Trading) — pp. 123–126

## Summary

In equity markets, OTM put options trade at significantly higher implied volatility than ATM options, while OTM calls trade at lower IV. This put skew exists because: (1) stocks have negative skewness in realized returns (crash risk), (2) investors pay a premium for tail protection, (3) risk-neutral and physical measures differ (jump risk). The put skew means selling OTM puts collects a volatility premium above what BSM fair value implies — but this extra premium compensates for real fat-tail crash risk.
