# True ITM Probability vs Risk-Neutral Probability: The Difference Matters

**Type:** mechanism
**Source:** Positional Option Trading (Wiley Trading) — pp. 151–152

## Summary

BSM's N(d₂) is the risk-neutral probability of expiring ITM — used only for hedging. The true (physical-measure) probability of expiring ITM is N(d₄) from the Boness model, where d₄ uses the actual drift μ. For a 1-year 110-call with S=$100, σ=30%, r=0%, μ=10%: risk-neutral probability = 32%, physical probability = 45% — a 13-point difference. Speculators should use N(d₄), not N(d₂), when assessing the probability of their option expiring profitably under their directional forecast.
