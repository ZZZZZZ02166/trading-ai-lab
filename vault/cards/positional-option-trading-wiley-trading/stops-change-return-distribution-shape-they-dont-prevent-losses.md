# Stops Change Return Distribution Shape — They Don't Prevent Losses

**Type:** caution
**Source:** Positional Option Trading (Wiley Trading) — pp. 198–200

## Summary

Adding a stop loss does not simply truncate the left tail of the return distribution. Simulation shows: stops eliminate some large losers but also remove many trades that would have recovered. Resulting distribution is bimodal — many small losses just above the stop and large winners that survive. Average return falls (stop-outs = realized losses on recovering trades). Trailing stops are worse than fixed stops — they eliminate even more winners. Stops are appropriate only when position monitoring is impossible or drawdown risk is existential.
