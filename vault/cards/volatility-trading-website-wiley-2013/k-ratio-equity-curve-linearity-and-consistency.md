# K Ratio: Equity Curve Linearity and Consistency

**Type:** definition
**Source:** Volatility Trading, + Website Wiley (2013) — pp. 183-183

## Summary

K Ratio (Kestner 1996, updated 2003): measures the consistency of returns by fitting a linear regression to the logarithmic equity curve and computing: K = slope / (std_error_of_slope × √T). Figures 9.6 and 9.7 contrast a 'linear' equity curve (high K) vs a 'nonlinear' one (same final return but higher K because the returns are more evenly distributed). A high K ratio means returns arrive steadily — the strategy is robust across time. A low K ratio (even with a high Sharpe) suggests the returns are concentrated in a few periods — possibly overfitted or luck-dependent. Sinclair recommends monitoring K over time: a declining K signals strategy decay.
