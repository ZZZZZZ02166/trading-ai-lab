# Variance Premium: Definition and Empirical Magnitude

**Type:** concept
**Source:** Volatility Trading, + Website Wiley (2013) — pp. 225-225

## Summary

The variance premium is the systematic tendency for equity index implied variance (IV²) to exceed subsequently realized variance. Empirically verified: on average, IV exceeds realized vol by 2-4 percentage points for equity indices. In the Sinclair study period (QQQ/QQQQ), the premium was consistent across option structures: straddles, strangles, and risk reversals all show positive expected P/L from selling. The premium is driven by: (1) negative skewness of index returns → investors pay risk premium for left-tail protection; (2) vol-of-vol (index vol itself is volatile, and uncertainty about vol commands a premium); (3) correlation risk — index puts hedge portfolio correlation spikes.
