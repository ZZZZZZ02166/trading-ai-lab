# IV is Mean-Reverting: VIX Regime Structure

**Type:** market_structure_insight
**Source:** Volatility Trading, + Website Wiley (2013) — pp. 87-87

## Summary

Figure 5.7 shows VIX time series 1990–2007: persistent regimes above 20 (high-vol) and below 20 (quiet). Sinclair's operational definition: 'a time series is mean-reverting if it can be profitably traded by methods that assume moves tend to reverse.' VIX satisfies this — fading VIX spikes above 30 and adding on further moves has positive expectation historically. But mean reversion with a shifting mean (Figures 3.5/3.6): the long-run mean itself drifts across regimes (~12% in 2004–2006, ~22% in 2002–2003, ~25+ in 2008). The mean to revert to is not fixed — it is itself slowly evolving.
