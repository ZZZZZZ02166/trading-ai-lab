# Variance Premium: IV Systematically Exceeds Realized Volatility

**Type:** concept
**Source:** Volatility Trading, + Website Wiley (2013) — pp. 75-75

## Summary

The variance premium is the persistent tendency of implied volatility (IV) to exceed subsequently realized volatility. On average, IV is approximately 30% higher than realized vol (i.e., if IV = 20%, realized vol tends to be ~15%). Equivalently, variance swaps always overpay: the fixed leg (IV²) exceeds the floating leg (realized variance) on average. This structural premium is the theoretical basis for selling options. Important: the premium varies over time and can be near zero or negative for months. High IV is necessary but not sufficient to guarantee a positive expected return from short vol — the premium must be positive relative to that IV level.
