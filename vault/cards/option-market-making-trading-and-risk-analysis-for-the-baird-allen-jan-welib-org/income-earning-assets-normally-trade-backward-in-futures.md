# Income-Earning Assets Normally Trade Backward in Futures

**Type:** principle
**Source:** Option Market Making — pp. 46–47

## Summary

For financial assets with positive income (stocks paying dividends, bonds paying coupons), futures prices are typically below spot — the market discounts the future price by the income yield, creating a normally backward term structure. This contrasts with non-income assets (like gold) where storage costs push futures above spot (contango). Understanding this helps set correct fair values for options on different asset types.
