# S&P 500 Returns Have Significantly Fatter Tails Than Normal

**Type:** volatility_insight
**Source:** Option Market Making — pp. 33–34

## Summary

[VISUAL: Figure 2.6] Comparing S&P 500 five-day returns from 1928-1988 to the normal curve reveals an error pattern: positive excess frequency in the extreme tails (beyond ±2 SD) and negative excess frequency (deficit) in the ±1 to ±2 SD region. The stock market's fat tails are not random noise — they are a persistent structural feature. Options pricing models that ignore this will systematically misprice tail risk and deep OTM options.
