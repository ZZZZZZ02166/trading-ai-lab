# Heteroscedasticity: Volatility is Not Constant Over Time

**Type:** caution
**Source:** Option Market Making — pp. 40–41

## Summary

Historical volatility is unreliable for predicting future volatility because volatility changes over time — a property called heteroscedasticity (variance of variance). Commodity volatility may be quiet for extended periods, then break into extreme price runs. Using one historical volatility period (e.g., 20-day) as a forecast for future volatility is inherently flawed. This is one of the most important sources of pricing uncertainty for option market makers.
