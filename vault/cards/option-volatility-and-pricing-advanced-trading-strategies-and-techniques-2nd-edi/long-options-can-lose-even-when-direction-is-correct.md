# Long Options Can Lose Even When Direction Is Correct

**Type:** principle
**Source:** Option Volatility and Pricing — pp. 430-438

## Summary

A critical principle: a long option position requires both correct direction AND sufficient speed of movement. If the underlying moves in the right direction but too slowly, time decay (theta) can exceed the value gained from the directional move, resulting in a net loss. This is the fundamental tradeoff of buying options vs. buying the underlying: options have capped risk but require the move to happen fast enough. The option buyer is paying for the right but also paying theta every day. Three-part forecast required: direction, magnitude, and timing. Getting one or two right while missing the third loses.
