# Futures Margining: Daily Mark-to-Market Settlement

**Type:** mechanism
**Source:** Option Volatility and Pricing — pp. 117-124

## Summary

Futures contracts require a margin deposit from both buyer and seller as a performance bond. Unlike stock margin (a loan), futures margin is collateral against default. Daily mark-to-market: each day, gains are credited and losses are debited to accounts. If losses reduce the account below the maintenance margin, a margin call is issued. This daily settlement is why futures positions can be held with relatively small capital vs. notional: losses are paid continuously rather than accumulating. Importantly, the daily settlement also means that futures options have 'futures-type settlement' — gains/losses on futures options are credited/debited daily.
