# Calendar Spread: Why It Benefits from Time Passage and Rising IV

**Type:** mechanism
**Source:** Option Volatility and Pricing — pp. 1381-1387

## Summary

A long calendar spread (short near-term, long far-term, same strike) increases in value when: (1) Time passes with underlying near the strike — the near-term option decays faster (ATM theta accelerates near expiry) while the far-term option decays more slowly; (2) IV rises — the far-term option has higher vega (more time), so the long side benefits more than the short side loses. The calendar spread loses value when: the underlying moves far from the strike (both options become OTM or ITM, reducing time premium difference), or IV falls.
