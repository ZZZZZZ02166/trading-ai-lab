# Risk Reversal: Synthetic Long/Short Underlying via Options

**Type:** definition
**Source:** Option Volatility and Pricing — pp. 3897-3907

## Summary

A risk reversal consists of a long call and short put (or vice versa) with the same underlying, expiration, but different strikes. A synthetic long underlying uses the same strike (equivalent to long stock). An actual risk reversal uses different strikes — creating net long or net short directional exposure with a specific skew structure. Long risk reversal (long call, short lower put): bullish with limited downside protection. Short risk reversal (short call, long lower put): bearish, equivalent to buying insurance. Risk reversals are used to express skew views: if you think OTM puts are overpriced relative to OTM calls, sell the put and buy the call — a short risk reversal collecting the skew premium.
