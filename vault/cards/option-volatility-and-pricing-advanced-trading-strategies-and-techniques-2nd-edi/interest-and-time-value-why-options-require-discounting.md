# Interest and Time Value: Why Options Require Discounting

**Type:** mechanism
**Source:** Option Volatility and Pricing — pp. 447-455

## Summary

Even if an option's expected payoff is known with certainty, its present value differs from its expected future value because of the time value of money. If a casino promises to pay $36 winnings two months from now rather than immediately, the present value of that $36 is less (e.g., $35.30 at 12% annual rate). Similarly, the theoretical value of an option is the present value of its expected payoff at expiration. For options with stock-type settlement, the option premium is paid upfront, so the full premium (not just intrinsic value) must be discounted. This is why options are worth more when interest rates are higher (for calls) — the cost of carry increases the forward price.
