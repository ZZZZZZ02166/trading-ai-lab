# Short Selling in Derivatives: Sell First, Buy Later

**Type:** definition
**Source:** Option Volatility and Pricing — pp. 98-108

## Summary

In conventional markets, selling requires prior ownership. Derivatives markets invert this: you can sell first and buy back later (short selling). The profit is identical regardless of order — only entry and exit prices matter. This ability to initiate a short position without prior ownership is foundational to: (1) options and futures trading; (2) arbitrage (conversions, reversals); (3) delta-neutral hedging; (4) spread strategies where one leg is short. Market makers depend on the ability to short options freely — they are frequently short the options they sell to customers.
