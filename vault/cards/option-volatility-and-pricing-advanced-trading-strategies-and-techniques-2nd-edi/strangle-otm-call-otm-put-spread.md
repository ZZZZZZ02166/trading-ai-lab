# Strangle: OTM Call + OTM Put Spread

**Type:** definition
**Source:** Option Volatility and Pricing — pp. 1306-1318

## Summary

A long strangle consists of a long OTM call and a long OTM put with different strikes (both out-of-the-money) at the same expiration. Greek profile: +gamma, −theta, +vega — identical in sign to a straddle. Differences from straddle: lower cost (both legs OTM), wider breakeven range at expiration, lower gamma and vega per dollar invested. A guts strangle uses ITM options instead of OTM options — equivalent P/L but different pricing due to intrinsic value. In practice, OTM strangles are more actively traded than ITM strangles because they require less capital and have lower absolute vega risk.
