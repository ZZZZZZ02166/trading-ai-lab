# Size vs. Risk: Butterflies Can Be Larger Positions with Less Risk

**Type:** principle
**Source:** Option Volatility and Pricing — pp. 1326-1337

## Summary

In options, size and risk do not always correlate. A trader can buy 300 butterflies (300 × 600 × 300) with less total risk than selling 100 straddles. Why: the butterfly has defined, limited risk (premium paid) with a large potential reward if the underlying pins near the body. The straddle has large gamma and vega risk that can create substantial losses if the market moves far. This counterintuitive relationship — where larger size has lower risk — is a fundamental property of spread positions. Managing risk by structure is often more important than managing risk by position size alone.
