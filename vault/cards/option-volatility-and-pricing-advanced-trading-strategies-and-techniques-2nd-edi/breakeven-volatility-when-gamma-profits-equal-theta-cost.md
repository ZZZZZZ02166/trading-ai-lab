# Breakeven Volatility: When Gamma Profits Equal Theta Cost

**Type:** mechanism
**Source:** Option Volatility and Pricing — pp. 1018-1025

## Summary

A long-gamma, delta-neutral position has a breakeven realized volatility. If realized vol exceeds this breakeven, gamma scalps > theta paid → profit. If realized vol falls short, theta paid > gamma scalps → loss. The breakeven vol is closely related to the implied volatility at which the options were purchased — if you buy at 30% IV, breakeven realized vol is approximately 30%. The more the underlying moves (higher realized vol), the more each rebalancing trade captures. In an extremely quiet market, gamma scalps are negligible and theta eats the position.
