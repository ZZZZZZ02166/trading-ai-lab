# Notional Value of Derivative Contracts

**Type:** definition
**Source:** Option Volatility and Pricing — pp. 108-117

## Summary

A derivative contract has no immediate cash exchange but carries a notional (nominal) value representing the total underlying exposure. For physical commodities: notional = quantity × unit price (e.g., 1,000 units × $75 = $75,000 notional). For cash-settled contracts (stock index futures), physical delivery is impractical — settlement occurs in cash. Notional value matters for: (1) margin requirement calculations (margin is a fraction of notional); (2) understanding true capital at risk vs. initial margin; (3) position sizing relative to portfolio value. A $10,000 margin deposit on a $100,000 notional contract provides 10x leverage.
