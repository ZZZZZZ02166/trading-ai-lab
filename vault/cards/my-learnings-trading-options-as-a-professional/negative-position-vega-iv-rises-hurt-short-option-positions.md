# Negative Position Vega: IV Rises Hurt Short-Option Positions

**Type:** caution
**Source:** My Learnings   Trading Options As A Professional — pp. 151-151

## Summary

Short options have negative position vega. Example: short 4 XYZ 80 Calls, vega 0.13 → position vega = −0.52. A 1% IV rise causes a $52 loss on this position. In practice, IV often rises during adverse market moves (fear increases uncertainty), meaning short-option traders suffer doubly: from delta (directional loss) and from vega (IV expansion loss) simultaneously. This is the compounding risk of short vol.
